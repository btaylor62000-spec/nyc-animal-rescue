# Community cats, colonies and starting TNR

_How trap-neuter-return works in New York City, and who will help you do it._

A community cat is an outdoor cat without a direct owner. Some are friendly (may be adoptable), most ferals are not - TNR + lifelong colony care is the humane, effective path.

## Step 1 - get TNR-certified (free; unlocks free spay/neuter, trap loans, transport)

- Neighborhood Cats TNR Certification Workshop - monthly online, certifies you as a Certified TNR Caretaker. neighborhoodcats.org/how-to-tnr. 15,000+ New Yorkers certified.
- NYC Feral Cat Initiative (FCI, run by Bideawee) - free TNR certification and colony-care workshops, and a 7-day helpdesk for Certified TNR Caretakers: https://www.bideawee.org/programs/feral-cat-initiative/ (helpdesk: https://nycfci.happyfox.com/new).
- Certification is what makes the free/low-cost spay/neuter, trap banks, and transport (see [Low-cost cat clinics and trap banks](/guides/low-cost-clinics-and-trap-banks)) available to you.

## Step 2 - register the colony (protects it + tracks progress)

- Cat Stats colony registry (Neighborhood Cats / Mayor's Alliance) - catstats.org/nyc - log your colony, cats, and spay/neuter status.
- Registration helps document TNR progress, coordinate with other caretakers, and can protect a colony's feeding site.

## Step 3 - trap and fix

- Borrow traps: Neighborhood Cats, ASPCA, FCI. Details: [Low-cost cat clinics and trap banks](/guides/low-cost-clinics-and-trap-banks)
- Free/low-cost spay/neuter for community cats: Toby Project (all 5 boroughs, free), ASPCA, plus feral-friendly clinics by borough.
- An EAR-TIP (left ear, squared off) = already spayed/neutered + rabies-vaccinated. An ear-tipped cat may still need to be trapped again, for vet care or in an emergency.

## Step 4 - ongoing colony care

- Winter shelters: Urban Cat League + FCI run shelter-building workshops (insulated, straw-not-blankets, small entrance). How to build one: Bideawee's guide, https://www.bideawee.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/CatShelter.pdf , and Alley Cat Allies' guide, https://www.alleycat.org/resources/how-to-build-an-outdoor-shelter/ . Ready-made shelters to buy: https://www.alleycat.org/resources/feral-cat-shelter-options-gallery/ .
- Food/water + discreet feeding stations that don't create odor/waste nuisance (keeps neighbors + the colony safe).
- Colony-support groups that help caretakers directly (with contacts): Bronx Community Cats (bronxcommunitycats.org), ARQ/Queens (arqadopt.org), Ocean Hill Cats/Brooklyn (info@oceanhillcats.com), Good Home/Brooklyn trap bank (info@goodhomepetadoptions.org), Mama Chris/SI (text 347-983-2240).

## Friendly cats / kittens found in a colony (pull for adoption)

- Socialize: Urban Cat League Taming Toolkit (in the guide [Lost or found a cat](/guides/lost-and-found-cat)). Under 8 weeks socialize easily; older takes longer.
- Neonatal/bottle-babies too young to survive outdoors: route to a nursery - Itty Bitty City Kitties, Bay Ridge Kittens, Kittens and Barbells. More: [Cats needing specialist help](/guides/cats-with-special-needs)

## When a cat can't be returned (site lost, unsafe, or truly unadoptable feral)

- Working-cat / barn relocation: Urban Cat League and For Animals Inc. place un-adoptable ferals as working cats (barns, warehouses, breweries).
- Hard Hat Cats - places working/feral cats at NYC businesses (has placed inside the Brooklyn Navy Yard) - hardhatcats@gmail.com.

## Feral medical / emergencies

- Sick/injured feral: a clinic experienced with feral cats ([Low-cost cat clinics and trap banks](/guides/low-cost-clinics-and-trap-banks)) or an emergency hospital ([Animal emergencies and poison control](/guides/animal-emergency)). Ring ahead and bring the cat in a covered trap.
- Dental care for a feral cat: see the dental section of [Low-cost cat clinics and trap banks](/guides/low-cost-clinics-and-trap-banks)
- Scratched or bitten by a feral? Contact a doctor immediately + report to NYC Health Animal Bite Unit (311). Handle only in a trap/transfer cage.
- KEY CONTACTS: FCI helpdesk nycfci.happyfox.com/new / (212) 330-0033 - Neighborhood Cats help@neighborhoodcats.org / (212) 662-5761 - these two are the backbone for any colony question.

## Services available for colony / community cats (what you can actually get, and who provides it)

Most free services require you to be a Certified TNR Caretaker (free to become - see Step 1). Register with the provider first.

## Spay/neuter + rabies + ear-tip (the core service, free for certified caretakers):

- Toby Project: FREE, all 5 boroughs. ASPCA: free for rescue groups through its Rescuer Services Scheduler. HSNY: low-cost. Every clinic: [Low-cost cat clinics and trap banks](/guides/low-cost-clinics-and-trap-banks)

## Free transport to/from spay-neuter (if you can't drive traps):

- ASPCA offers free transport to/from its clinics - book with the spay/neuter appointment. Cats returned to the same pickup spot next day.

## Free/loaned traps + drop-traps:

- Neighborhood Cats trap bank (pickup 30 Prince St, Brooklyn), ASPCA trap bank (TNRtrapbank@aspca.org), FCI loans.

## Free cat food, straw & winter shelters for colonies:

- NYC Feral Cat Initiative (FCI) runs periodic giveaways of free cat food, STRAW (best shelter bedding - insulates, wicks moisture), and pre-made winter shelters to Certified TNR Caretakers. Ask the FCI helpdesk about current giveaways.
- Straw (NOT hay, NOT blankets which hold cold/damp) is the correct insulator; FCI + Neighborhood Cats have build guides + sometimes distribute shelters.
- ASPCA + Food Bank pet-food pantries (all boroughs) can also supply colony feeding.

## Free posting of adoptable colony cats (friendlies/kittens pulled from the colony):

- Ask the FCI helpdesk (https://nycfci.happyfox.com/new) whether it can post your friendly colony cats or kittens for adoption: it has listed Certified TNR Caretakers' adoptable cats before, though Bideawee's FCI page (https://www.bideawee.org/programs/feral-cat-initiative/) no longer says so. Borough rescues in the directory also take friendlies into their adoption programmes.

## Grants / financial help for TNR + colony vet costs:

- FCI has run grants for individuals' TNR and adoption costs (from the ASPCA and In Defense of Animals). Ask its helpdesk what is open now: https://nycfci.happyfox.com/new. Help with vet bills: [Help paying for veterinary care](/guides/help-paying-for-care)

## Medical for colony cats (sick/injured/dental):

- Feral-friendly clinics + emergency hospitals + feral dentals: see [Low-cost cat clinics and trap banks](/guides/low-cost-clinics-and-trap-banks) and [Animal emergencies and poison control](/guides/animal-emergency). Trap the cat, bring it covered.

## Community-relations help (neighbor complaints, feeding disputes, moving a feeding station):

- FCI and Neighborhood Cats offer guidance and the 'Love Cats / Hate Cats' outreach materials. Alley Cat Allies' advice on neighbours and complaints: https://www.alleycat.org/community-cat-care-category/community-relations/ (start with What to Do if Neighbors Complain: https://www.alleycat.org/community-cat-care/making-nice-with-the-neighbors/). NY State classifies cats (incl. feral) as companion animals - caretaking and feeding is legal, though local feeding rules can vary.

## Hands-on help (someone to trap with you, holding space, fosters):

- Borough TNR groups that trap alongside you / take friendlies / hold (with contacts): Bronx Community Cats (bronxcommunitycats.org), ARQ (arqadopt.org), Ocean Hill Cats (info@oceanhillcats.com), Good Home (info@goodhomepetadoptions.org), Mama Chris SI (text 347-983-2240), Bushwick Street Cats (bushwickstreetcats@gmail.com), North Brooklyn Cats (northbrooklyncats.org/help), Flatbush Cats (help@flatbushcats.org). Match to the colony's neighborhood.
