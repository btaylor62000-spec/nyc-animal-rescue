# Found injured or orphaned wildlife

_Only licensed rehabilitators may legally handle wild animals. Where to take them, and what not to do._

> Wildlife rehabilitation - critical and legally distinct

The rule first: only NYS-licensed wildlife rehabilitators may legally handle wild animals in New York, and most wild animals cannot legally be kept as pets. Wildlife is a rehabilitation-and-release matter, NOT an adoption matter. If you find injured, sick or orphaned wildlife, contact a licensed rehabber for advice before handling it - some species need special handling and some carry diseases transmissible to people.

NYC's main wildlife hospital - The Wild Bird Fund

565 Columbus Avenue (between 87th & 88th), New York, NY 10024. Phone 646-306-2862. Open daily 9am-7pm. NYC's only wildlife rehabilitation & education center. Takes injured/sick/orphaned wild birds AND small mammals (pigeons, waterfowl, raptors, songbirds, squirrels, opossums) 7 days a week; walk-in, no appointment needed (leave a message if arriving near closing). Found-a-bird guidance: wildbirdfund.org/how-to-help/rescue. IG @wildbirdfund.

LIMITATION: WBF CANNOT take exotic birds (budgerigars, parrots) or domestic birds (chickens, quail, domestic geese, tame doves). It CAN take rock/domestic pigeons. For exotic or domestic birds WBF can't take, Animal Care Centers of NYC (311 / 212-788-4000) is the drop-off (as of 2022): chukars, chickens, domestic geese, budgerigars, etc.

Rabies Vector Species - raccoons, skunks, bats (important NYC limitation)

There are NO NYC-licensed wildlife rehabilitators who can take raccoons, skunks or bats, because these are classified as Rabies Vector Species (RVS) in New York and require a special RVS license. Licensed RVS rehabbers exist in surrounding counties - find them via the DEC rehabber search (select 'bats, raccoons, or skunks' and search county by county). For an RVS animal in the city: stay away, do not touch or feed it, call 311 and ask for the Urban Park Rangers, and file the parks online report; call 911 too if there's a risk to people or the animal. If you've had contact with an RVS animal, contact your doctor immediately. Any mammal can carry rabies. (For coyote questions: Gotham Coyote Project - seeing a coyote in NYC is usually not cause for alarm.)

What to do / what NOT to do with an injured wild animal

DO: note the exact location; if safe, contain a small bird/mammal in a ventilated box in a warm, dark, quiet place; keep it away from pets and children; call a licensed rehabber or the Wild Bird Fund promptly. Do NOT offer food or water unless a rehabber tells you to.

DON'T: try to raise or keep wildlife yourself (illegal and usually fatal for the animal); handle bats, raccoons or other rabies-vector species with bare hands (call DEC/animal control); or assume a lone fawn or fledgling is orphaned - many are not, so check with a rehabber first.
