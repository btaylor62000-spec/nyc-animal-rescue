# Found injured or orphaned wildlife

_Squirrels, rabbits, raccoons, deer, turtles and seals: who to call, and what not to do. Birds have their own guide._

## Who to call right now

Do not pick up a wild mammal with bare hands, and do not give any wild animal food or water. Then:

- **A bird:** see [Found an injured bird](/guides/found-an-injured-bird). Birds are the exception to "do not touch": catch it and box it straight away.
- **Squirrel, opossum, rabbit or other small mammal, hurt or sick:** [The Wild Bird Fund](/org/the-wild-bird-fund) takes small mammals too. 565 Columbus Ave, Manhattan, any day 9am–7pm, no appointment. (646) 306-2862.
- **Wild baby rabbit, fox or other wild mammal, or the Wild Bird Fund is closed:** [Cottontail Cottage](/org/cottontail-cottage-wildlife-rehab), (914) 933-7559, any hour (leave a message; they call back). Or [Animal Help Now](/org/animal-help-now-wildlife-emergency-finder), which shows the licensed rehabbers near the address, those available now first, or the [DEC directory](/org/nys-dec-find-a-wildlife-rehabilitator).
- **Raccoon, skunk or bat:** do not touch it. No rehabber in the city is licensed to take these. Call 311 and ask for the Urban Park Rangers; call 911 if a person is at risk. If a person or a pet has touched it, call a doctor or vet now.
- **Seal, whale, dolphin or sea turtle on a beach:** keep your distance, keep dogs away, and call [the stranding hotline](/org/ny-marine-mammal-and-sea-turtle-stranding-hotline), (631) 369-9829; if it does not answer, [NOAA](/org/noaa-greater-atlantic-marine-mammal-and-sea-turtle-stranding-hotline), (866) 755-6622.
- **A wild turtle that is hurt:** [the New York Turtle and Tortoise Society](/org/new-york-turtle-and-tortoise-society), qanda@nytts.org.
- **Deer, coyote or other large animal:** leave it be unless it is badly hurt or on a road; then [DEC Region 2](/org/nys-dec-region-2), (718) 482-4900 in the daytime, or 911.
- **Someone keeping, selling or killing protected wildlife:** [the federal tip line](/org/usfws-wildlife-crime-tip-line), (844) 397-8477, anonymously if you like, or [DEC Region 2](/org/nys-dec-region-2).

The rule first: only NYS-licensed wildlife rehabilitators may legally handle wild animals in New York, and most wild animals cannot legally be kept as pets. Wildlife is a rehabilitation-and-release matter, NOT an adoption matter. If you find injured, sick or orphaned wildlife, call a licensed rehabber for advice before you touch a wild mammal - some species need special handling and some carry diseases people can catch. Birds are the exception: a hurt bird should be caught and boxed straight away, because it may fly off in shock and die later. How to do that is in [Found an injured bird](/guides/found-an-injured-bird).

## Found a baby squirrel or rabbit?

- If it is hurt, cold, covered in flies, or was caught by an animal, it needs a rehabber today: the Wild Bird Fund takes squirrels and other small mammals, and Cottontail Cottage takes wild baby rabbits. If it seems healthy and its nest has fallen, its mother may come back for it: Animal Help Now explains how to give her the chance, https://ahnow.org/resources.php#babysquirrel and https://ahnow.org/resources.php#babymammal. Urban Utopia Wildlife Rehabilitation is on hiatus from rehab but keeps its reunite instructions online: https://www.urbanutopiawildlife.org/reunite

## The city's wildlife hospital: the Wild Bird Fund

- 565 Columbus Avenue (between 87th & 88th), New York, NY 10024. Phone 646-306-2862. Open daily 9am-7pm. NYC's only wildlife rehabilitation & education center. Takes injured/sick/orphaned wild birds AND small mammals (pigeons, waterfowl, raptors, songbirds, squirrels, opossums) 7 days a week; walk-in, no appointment needed (leave a message if arriving near closing). Found-a-bird guidance: wildbirdfund.org/how-to-help/rescue. Instagram @wildbirdfund.
- LIMITATION: WBF CANNOT take exotic birds (budgerigars, parrots) or domestic birds (chickens, quail, domestic geese, tame doves). It CAN take rock/domestic pigeons. For exotic or domestic birds WBF can't take, Animal Care Centers of NYC (311 / 212-788-4000) is the drop-off (as of 2022): chukars, chickens, domestic geese, budgerigars, etc.

## Raccoons, skunks and bats: no rehabber in the city may take them

There are NO NYC-licensed wildlife rehabilitators who can take raccoons, skunks or bats, because these are classified as Rabies Vector Species (RVS) in New York and require a special RVS license. Licensed RVS rehabbers exist in surrounding counties - find them via the DEC rehabber search (select 'bats, raccoons, or skunks' and search county by county). For an RVS animal in the city: stay away, do not touch or feed it, call 311 and ask for the Urban Park Rangers, and file the parks online report; call 911 too if there's a risk to people or the animal. If you've had contact with an RVS animal, contact your doctor immediately. Any mammal can carry rabies. (For coyote questions: Gotham Coyote Project - seeing a coyote in NYC is usually not cause for alarm.)

## Seals, whales, dolphins and sea turtles

- NY Marine Mammal & Sea Turtle Stranding Hotline: 631-369-9829 (24 hours), operated by the New York Marine Rescue Center (Riverhead), the only NY facility permitted to rehabilitate marine mammals and sea turtles. Keep your distance, keep dogs away, and call - do NOT attempt to move the animal. A seal resting ('hauled out') on a beach is often healthy; reporting lets trained responders assess it. Program contact: mmontello@nymarinerescue.org.

## Wild turtles

- For sick/injured WILD native turtles, NYTTS's turtle-rehab program has rehabbed NYC turtles for 30+ years: email qanda@nytts.org. For PET turtles needing rehoming, see the reptiles rows of the main directory (NYTTS, Earthlings NYC). To report illegal sale of NY native reptiles/amphibians: DEC tips line 1-800-847-7332.

## Can't take an injured bird there yourself?

- Email the Urban Wildlife Alliance helpline: helpline@urbanwildlifenyc.org (urbanwildlifenyc.org), with a short description, a photo or video, your phone number and your location. NYC Bird Alliance, whose page sends people there, warns that you may not get a reply the same day and that a volunteer cannot always come, so you or a friend are often the animal's best chance. NYC Bird Alliance also maintains an authoritative NYC injured-bird routing page (all listed rehabbers accept birds; many take other wildlife): https://nycbirdalliance.org/take-action/help-a-bird-in-trouble/nyc-region-animal-hospitals-and-rehabilitation-centers.

## What to do, and what not to do

DO: note the exact location; if safe, contain a small bird/mammal in a ventilated box in a dark, quiet place in a warm room (no heating pad for a bird that hit a window; a cold, nearly naked baby bird is the exception, see the bird guide); keep it away from pets and children; call a licensed rehabber or the Wild Bird Fund promptly. Do NOT offer food or water.

DON'T: try to raise or keep wildlife yourself (illegal and usually fatal for the animal); handle bats, raccoons or other rabies-vector species with bare hands (call DEC/animal control); or assume a lone fawn or fledgling is orphaned - many are not, so check with a rehabber first.

## Finding a licensed rehabber near you

- Most of the city's licensed wildlife rehabilitators are individuals caring for animals at home. They are not listed one by one here; the organizations and hospitals are, below. To reach a home rehabber near you, use Animal Help Now: https://ahnow.org - type the address where the animal is and tap Wildlife Emergency. It shows each licensed rehabber nearby, the animals they take and their phone number (tap a listing), with those available right now first, kept current from the state's licence lists.
- Or New York State's own register of licensed rehabilitators (DEC): https://appfactory.dec.ny.gov/SpecialLicensesSearchSystem/rehab - choose the county (Bronx; Kings for Brooklyn; New York for Manhattan; Queens; Richmond for Staten Island) and the kind of animal.

## The state's register of rehabbers (DEC)

- Searchable by county and species: appfactory.dec.ny.gov/SpecialLicensesSearchSystem/rehab (overview: dec.ny.gov/nature/wildlife-health/rehabilitators). DEC general/after-hours line: (844) 332-3267. Not every rehabber can accept every animal, and many run at capacity in spring/summer - if one is full, try the next-nearest listing. Species like deer, raccoons, bats and federally protected birds need special permits and have very few licensees.

## A second directory (NYS Wildlife Rehabilitation Council)

- 'Find a Wildlife Professional': nyswrc.org/find-a-wildlife-professional. Lists NYC-area rehabbers including Urban Utopia Wildlife Rehabilitation (NYC) and WINORR - Wildlife In Need Of Rescue and Rehabilitation (NYC / Nassau / Suffolk). Contact details rotate with licensing; confirm current phone/email through this directory or the DEC search.

## More situations - Animal Help Now's guidance, in its own words

- Is it really orphaned? "Always remember that baby wild animals left alone for long periods of time (up to 14 hours) may not have been abandoned. Do not interfere unless you are certain the animal has been abandoned or separated from her mother. ... If the mother is confirmed dead or the baby animal is covered with blood or insects, has been caught by [another animal] or is cold, use Animal Help Now to contact a nearby wildlife rehabilitator."
- Baby duck, goose or other ground-nesting bird: "If you know the mother is dead, or if the baby is injured, secure the bird in a safe, well-ventilated box or paper sack and call a wildlife rehabilitator right away. If the baby is separated from the mother and you know where the mother is, place the baby close by so the mother can hear her. If the mother does not claim the baby within an hour, secure the bird in a safe, well-ventilated box or paper sack and contact a wildlife rehabilitator."
- Injured deer: "Adult deer don't handle the stress of capture and captivity well ... If the deer can walk and is in a relatively safe place, simply leave the deer alone. ... do not approach the deer - injured and scared deer could easily injure you or make her injury worse. If she is severely injured or near a busy road, contact a wildlife emergency professional through Animal Help Now for advice, or call 911 or Animal Control."
- Stuck in a glue trap, or with mange: Animal Help Now has step-by-step guidance for both: https://ahnow.org/resources.php#gluetrap and https://ahnow.org/resources.php#animalmange. Do not pull an animal off a glue trap; call a rehabber.
- A wild animal used to people: "first and foremost be sure to eliminate any food sources that may be attracting the animal. Observe, too, to ensure the animal does not have dependent young. ... hazing is often the most humane way to help." Keeping a wild animal at home is illegal in New York.
- A bird with a band on its leg: if it is hurt, get it help first. "The USFWS Migratory Bird Program and researchers rely on the public to report federally banded birds and those with auxiliary markers at www.reportband.gov." Racing-pigeon bands can be reported to the American Racing Pigeon Union.
- An animal in the road while you are driving: "tap your brakes (so your brake lights flash), sound your horn, and flash your headlights to scare off the animal and to warn other drivers behind and in front of you."
- Every other situation Animal Help Now answers (baby squirrels, bats, turtles, a pet that caught a wild animal, how to capture and transport, wildlife conflicts and more), each linked to its answer: [More situations, answered by Animal Help Now](/guides/more-situations-animal-help-now).
