# Reporting animal abuse or neglect

_Who to call in New York City, what to write down, and why it is the police rather than the ASPCA._

Since 2014 the NYPD, not the ASPCA, leads all animal cruelty response in New York City. When you call 311 or 911 your report goes to the police. Calling the ASPCA to report cruelty will send you back to 311.

The ASPCA still works behind the scenes - forensic exams, medical care, housing for victims, and support to prosecutors - but it is not who you call first.

## IS THIS HAPPENING RIGHT NOW?

- Call 911 if an animal or a person is in danger right now, or a crime is happening as you watch. For example: an animal being beaten, a dogfight, an animal left in a hot or freezing car, an attack - or anything else happening in front of you that cannot wait. 911 is also correct any time a person is at risk.
- Call 311, or report online at portal.311.nyc.gov, for anything else, so it is on the record. For example: a chained dog with no shelter, repeated cruelty, a hoarding situation, an animal denied food, water or vet care - or anything else that worries you. You do not have to be sure it counts as cruelty. 311 routes your report to the NYPD precinct that covers that location. The city's own advice is the same: https://www.nyc.gov/site/animalwelfare/resources/animal-cruelty.page
- Then, if you want more people to know, write to your City Council member and to the Council's Animal Welfare Caucus, and to the Mayor's Office of Animal Welfare - see "Who to contact as well: the people who make the rules" below. They do not investigate, so do this as well as 311 or 911, never instead.

## HOW TO REPORT SO IT ACTUALLY GETS ACTED ON

- Report while it is happening if you safely can. The sooner you report, the more likely police can locate the animal and the person, and see the conditions themselves. Delay is the single biggest reason reports go nowhere.
- Give the exact location and specifics: address or cross streets, a description of the animal, a description of the person, and what you saw. You do not have to prove cruelty, only reasonably suspect it. Reporting in good faith is protected.
- Document before, or alongside, reporting. Photos, video, dates and times, and a written note of what you witnessed each time. A report backed by dated evidence is far harder to close without action.
- You can report anonymously. 311 and Crime Stoppers both take anonymous reports. Giving your contact details is optional but helps investigators follow up and can matter for prosecution. Choose based on your safety.
- Expect it to be slow, and keep a record. The NYPD does not share details of open investigations, so you often will not see what happens. That does not mean nothing did. If abuse continues, report again with your new dated observations; a pattern strengthens the case.

## WHAT TO WRITE DOWN - capture this for every incident

- Date and time you witnessed it.
- Exact location: address or nearest cross streets.
- The animal: species, breed if known, colour, size, condition.
- The person: description, and their relationship to the animal if known.
- What you saw, in plain factual terms: what was done, or what was missing (food, water, shelter, vet care).
- How many times you have seen it.
- Photos or video if you can capture them safely, without trespassing or confronting anyone.
- Names of any other witnesses.

## WHO TO CONTACT

- NYC 311 - 311 - portal.311.nyc.gov - ongoing abuse or neglect, non-emergency; routes to your NYPD precinct. The online report lets you upload photos, video and documents. Takes anonymous reports.
- NYPD Crime Stoppers - (800) 577-8477 - crimestoppers.nypdonline.org - anonymous tip on ongoing abuse, fighting, or a specific location. Also reachable as 800-577-TIPS. Fully anonymous tip line and online form.
- Your local NYPD precinct - nyc.gov/site/nypd/bureaus/patrol/find-your-precinct.page - report in-progress cruelty directly and ask for the ASPCA Liaison Officer. If a precinct says it has none, ask for the Neighborhood Coordination Officer or the Community Affairs Officer and say you want to file an animal abuse complaint. Not anonymous.
- HSUS dogfighting tip line - animalfighting@hsus.org - https://www.humaneworld.org/en/report-animal-cruelty-contact-form - suspected organised dogfighting or cockfighting that is not happening right now. The HSUS is now called Humane World for Animals; its current reward flyer says to email tips to animalfighting@hsus.org, and it has an online report form. Humane World offers a standing reward of $5,000 for information leading to a conviction for dogfighting or other animal fighting. For fighting in progress call 911. Your identity is protected.
- ASPCA Community Engagement - for a struggling owner who needs help with food, vet care or supplies rather than punishment. Agencies and the public can refer, by email to referrals@aspca.org. Helps keep pets with their owners when the problem is hardship, not cruelty.

## WHO TO CONTACT AS WELL: THE PEOPLE WHO MAKE THE RULES

These offices do not investigate cruelty and cannot send anyone out. Contact them after 311 or 911, to make sure the problem is known to the people who make the rules and decide the budgets. Say where and when it happened, give your 311 service request number, and attach what you documented.

- Your City Council member - https://council.nyc.gov/districts/ - the elected member for the district where it happened (not necessarily where you live). Enter the address on that page to find the district; each member's page gives the office's email and phone. Each of the 51 districts has its own member.
- NYC Council Animal Welfare Caucus - kgomez@council.nyc.gov - https://council.nyc.gov/caucuses/animal-welfare-caucus/ - the Council members who work on animal welfare law and funding. The Council publishes this email as the caucus's contact for questions and concerns.
- NYC Mayor's Office of Animal Welfare - animalwelfare@cityhall.nyc.gov - https://www.nyc.gov/site/animalwelfare/about/contact-us.page - the Mayor's office that coordinates city agencies on animal welfare. The office's head has said publicly that requests for help should go to this address. It is a very small office, so do not expect a quick reply; for anything urgent call 311 or 911. The contact page also has a web form.

## WHO TO CONTACT ABOUT A BUSINESS: A PET SHOP, BREEDER, SHELTER OR RESCUE

If the problem is with how a business or a rescue keeps or sells animals, report it to the state as well. If an animal is being hurt, call 311 or 911 first: the state says cruelty is handled locally, not by it.

- New York State Department of Agriculture and Markets - https://agriculture.ny.gov/animals/animal-related-complaints - complaints about the care, premises, running or records of a licensed pet dealer (a pet shop or a breeder) or of a shelter or rescue: https://agriculture.ny.gov/animals/pet-dealer-and-shelter-rescue-complaint-form . Complaints about someone buying, selling or rescuing farm animals without a permit, a reportable disease, or a broken quarantine order: https://agriculture.ny.gov/form/domestic-animal-health-permit-bu . Not for cruelty: the Department says cruelty is handled by local police and SPCAs.
- Better Business Bureau - https://www.bbb.org/file-a-complaint - a complaint about any business, for example a groomer, a boarding kennel, a pet shop or a breeder. For a seller who turned out to be fake, use the same page's "Report a Scam" option.

## WHO TO CONTACT ABOUT SOMETHING ONLINE

Report the post to the website or app it is on as well, and keep a copy of the link. If you know where in New York City the animal is, call 311, or 911 if it is happening now.

- FBI tips - (212) 384-1000 - https://tips.fbi.gov - videos or posts showing animals being deliberately tortured or killed. Making or sharing these is a federal crime. (212) 384-1000 is the FBI's New York office, which takes reports 24 hours a day; the national line is 1-800-CALL-FBI.
- FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) - https://www.ic3.gov - online scams, for example paying for a puppy or kitten that never existed, or for "shipping" that never came. IC3 is for fraud and scams only; it sends other crimes to tips.fbi.gov.

## IF PEOPLE ARE AT RISK TOO

Animal abuse often occurs alongside domestic violence, child abuse and elder abuse. If you are afraid for your own safety or someone else's, or an abuser is using a pet to threaten or control you, use these lines. Many domestic violence shelters can now keep pets safe too, so fear of leaving an animal behind does not have to trap someone. If anyone is in immediate danger, call 911.

- NYC Domestic Violence Hotline - (800) 621-4673 - nyc.gov/content/endgbv/pages/hotlines - abuse of a person alongside a pet, safety planning, leaving safely. Also reachable as 800-621-HOPE. 24/7, confidential, multilingual. Takes anonymous calls.
- URI PALS - (646) 588-0030 - urinyc.org/uripals - for a survivor who needs to leave but cannot abandon a pet. A New York City shelter programme that houses survivors together with their pets: dogs, cats and small animals.
- Safe Horizon - (212) 577-7700 - https://www.safehorizon.org - works with animal shelters, foster homes and boarding to keep a survivor's pet safe while they leave.
- Volunteers of America-Greater New York - (855) 643-7473 - https://www.voa-gny.org - domestic violence shelters where pets and people stay together.
- Safe Havens for Pets (Animal Welfare Institute) - https://www.safehavensforpets.org - every place that keeps pets safe for people leaving abuse, searchable by zip code.
- Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (988) - 988 - 988lifeline.org - if you or someone else needs mental health or crisis support. Free, confidential, multilingual, 24/7. New York City routes its mental health crisis line through 988. Takes anonymous calls.
- ASPCA Animal Poison Control - (888) 426-4435 - aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control - if an animal has been poisoned or exposed to a toxin. Staffed 24/7, 365 days a year.
