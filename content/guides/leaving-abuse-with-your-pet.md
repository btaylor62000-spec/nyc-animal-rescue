# Leaving an abusive home with your pet

_Shelters in New York City where survivors and pets stay together, keeping your pet safe while you get to safety, planning for your pet, and how a court can protect it._

If someone at home is hurting you, or threatening your pet to control you, you do not have to choose between your safety and your animal. New York City has shelters where survivors and pets live together, programmes that keep a pet safe while you get to safety, and a law that lets a court protect your pet. Advocates on the hotline below help you plan for your pet as part of leaving.

## IF YOU ARE IN DANGER NOW

- Call 911.
- NYC 24-hour Domestic Violence Hotline: (800) 621-4673, answered by Safe Horizon. Say that you have a pet and need to leave safely with it.
- National Domestic Violence Hotline: (800) 799-7233, or text START to 88788. If you think someone checks your phone or computer, call rather than browse, and clear your history afterwards.

## SHELTERS WHERE YOU AND YOUR PET CAN STAY TOGETHER

- URI PALS (People and Animals Living Safely) - run by the Urban Resource Institute, "the first and only program in New York City offering co-living arrangements" for survivors and their pets, in pet-ready apartments in three boroughs; it now runs in 11 shelters. Ask the hotline for a PALS placement, or call PALS.
- More of the city's domestic violence shelters take pets than used to: in December 2025, 17 of the city's 54 accepted pets. Always ask.
- Volunteers of America-Greater New York - runs domestic violence shelters where pets and people stay together.
- To search for any shelter or programme that keeps pets safe, by zip code: Safe Havens for Pets, from the Animal Welfare Institute, https://safehavensforpets.org, and DomesticShelters.org, which marks the programmes that take pets.

## IF YOUR PET CANNOT COME WITH YOU YET

- Safe Horizon works with animal shelters, foster homes and boarding to keep a survivor's pet safe. Ask when you call the hotline.
- Domestic violence shelters in the city work with foster homes that care for a pet for a while and then return it to you (the Safe Haven programme). A caseworker or the hotline can connect you.
- PAWS NY arranges emergency fostering for survivors: referrals@pawsny.org, (212) 203-4760 ext. 306.
- Helping Pets and People in Crisis, from the Mayor's Alliance for NYC's Animals, boards the pets of New Yorkers in a crisis.
- Animal Haven helps people at risk of losing their animal because of domestic violence.
- RedRover Relief's Safe Escape grants help pay for boarding while a survivor gets to safety. Ask your advocate to apply.
- Before you hand your pet to anyone, ask how long they will keep it, what happens if you cannot take it back in time, and whether you could lose ownership. Get the answers in writing.

## PLANNING FOR YOUR PET

- The National Domestic Violence Hotline's advice, in its own words (https://www.thehotline.org/resources/safety-planning-with-pets/):
- "Take steps to prove ownership of your pet. Have them vaccinated and licensed in the place where you live, making sure the registrations are done in your name." In New York City, dogs are licensed by the Health Department.
- "If possible, avoid leaving pets alone with an abusive partner."
- "If your pet is microchipped, make sure your abusive partner is not listed as a contact."
- "If you're planning to leave, talk to friends, family, or your veterinarian about temporary care for your pet if necessary."
- "If you decide to leave, bring extra provisions for your pets including food and medications, copies of their medical records, and important phone numbers."
- "After leaving, consider changing veterinarians and avoid leaving pets outside alone to ensure their long-term safety."

## A COURT CAN PROTECT YOUR PET

- In New York, an order of protection can include your pet: since 2006 the court may order the abuser to stay away from, and not harm, a companion animal belonging to you or your children (Family Court Act section 842, and Criminal Procedure Law section 530.12 in criminal court). Ask for it when you apply, and tell your advocate. How to apply for an order of protection: https://www.nycourts.gov/courthelp/safety/orderOfProtection.shtml

## IF YOUR PET HAS BEEN HURT, OR HAD TO STAY BEHIND

- Hurting or threatening an animal is a crime in New York, and in New York City the police handle it: call 911 if it is happening now. How to report it, and what to write down, is in [Reporting animal abuse or neglect](/guides/report-animal-abuse).
- If you had to leave your pet with the abuser, tell your advocate and the police. Do not go back alone to fetch it.
