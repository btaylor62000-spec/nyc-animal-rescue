# Save an animal from the shelter’s at-risk list

_How to get an at-risk animal out of the city shelter, who can actually pull it, and what to say._

New York City's public animal shelter is Animal Care Centers of NYC (ACC). When an animal there urgently needs to leave, ACC puts it on an “at-risk list”. Many of these animals cannot be adopted directly by a member of the public. They can only leave through an approved rescue group, which ACC calls a “New Hope partner”.

So if you want to save a particular at-risk animal, you usually cannot do it on your own. You need a New Hope partner to take the animal, with you lined up as the foster or adopter. This page is about reaching those partners fast.

## HOW MUCH TIME YOU HAVE

ACC posts the at-risk list three times a week, on Sunday, Tuesday and Thursday evenings at 6:00 PM. Each posting stays viewable for 42 hours, until 12:00 noon two days later: Sunday's list closes Tuesday noon, Tuesday's closes Thursday noon, Thursday's closes Saturday noon. Act before the listing closes.

ACC states that being on the at-risk list does not mean an animal is automatically euthanised. Euthanasia happens only when no placement has been secured. The reason to act fast is to secure that placement while the animal is still listed.

## DO THIS NOW, IN ORDER

- Confirm the animal is still listed. Find it on ACC's live at-risk list, linked below, and note its name and A-number. The list also shows whether it is already reserved.
- Submit ACC's pre-screener, linked below. This tells ACC you have a home, and ACC forwards your details to rescue partners. Do this even though you are also doing the next step. Note that the pre-screener is a Google form and asks you to sign in with a Google account; if you do not have one to hand, go straight to contacting partners and come back to it.
- Contact partners yourself, at the same time. Using the list below, contact every partner that takes your animal's species, through every channel each one lists, in parallel. Do not wait for one reply before trying the next, and do not assume a phone call is faster than a form or an email. Use the wording below so you are not composing a message under pressure.
- Keep going until a partner confirms the pull with ACC. A promising reply is not a pull. The partner has to notify ACC and complete their paperwork to take the animal. Only stop once that is confirmed.

## WHAT TO SAY

Fill in the brackets. The committed-home line is the one that gets a yes.

By phone, or as a voicemail: Hi, I'm calling about an at-risk animal at ACC. Name [ ], A-number [ ], at the [borough] centre, listed until [deadline]. [I am / I have] a committed foster or adopter ready the moment the animal is pulled. Can your organisation pull it as a New Hope partner? I'll complete any application right away and arrange transport. Please call or text me back at [your number].

By email, subject line: Urgent at-risk animal, committed home ready - A-number [ ], listed until [deadline]

By email, body: Name [ ], A-number [ ], [borough] centre, listed until [deadline]. [I am / There is] a committed foster or adopter ready the moment the animal is pulled. Can your organisation pull it as a New Hope partner? I'll complete any application immediately and arrange transport. Reach me at [phone] or [email].

Through an online form, kept short because forms cap the length: At-risk animal, A-number [ ], [borough] centre, listed until [deadline]. [I am / I have] a committed foster or adopter ready now. Can you pull it as a New Hope partner? I'll complete your application and arrange transport immediately. Contact: [phone] / [email].
