# Pets in a disaster or evacuation

_Evacuation shelters in New York City take pets. What to pack, how to get there, extreme heat, and first aid._

What New York City Emergency Management tells pet owners to do before and during an emergency: a storm, a flood, a fire in the building or extreme heat. The city's guidance below is from its animal planning manager (Prep Talk, episode 72, 2021: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/em/downloads/pdf/podcast_transcripts/prep_talk_72_preparing_pets_service_animals_for_disasters.pdf). Plans change, so check nyc.gov/readyny or call 311 when an emergency is announced.

## IF YOU HAVE TO EVACUATE

- Take your pet with you. New York City Emergency Management says: “New York City's emergency evacuation shelters are pet-friendly.” It also says the most common misconception is that people cannot bring their pets, and that it never wants anyone to stay home where it is dangerous just to be with their pet.
- “If an evacuation order is declared, pets are allowed on public transportation, even if they are not in a carrier.” Service animals are always allowed wherever people are allowed.
- Find out whether you live in an evacuation zone, and where your nearest evacuation centre is, at https://www.nyc.gov/knowyourzone or by calling 311.
- Get emergency alerts from Notify NYC, the city's free alert service (app, text or email): https://www.nyc.gov/notifynyc

## GET READY NOW

- Make a plan: write down your pet's details and your emergency contacts somewhere you can reach in an emergency, and decide where you would go and how you would get there with your pet.
- Pack a Go Bag for your pet, like your own: what your pet needs every day for at least three days away from home.
- Dogs and cats should wear a collar or harness, a rabies tag and an identification tag at all times. The ID tag should give your name, address and phone number, and the phone number of an emergency contact.
- Keep a current colour photo of your pet. It matters most if you go to an evacuation shelter with your pet.
- A microchip only helps if it is registered with your current phone number: see https://nyc-animal-rescue.pages.dev/guides/lost-and-found-dog
- The city's workbook, Ready New York: My Pet's Emergency Plan, walks through every step. It is available in 12 languages and as audio: https://www.nyc.gov/readyny or call 311.
- Federal guidance, with a full kit list: https://www.ready.gov/pets
- For birds, reptiles, rabbits and other small animals, which need heat, light or special food: see the disaster section of https://nyc-animal-rescue.pages.dev/guides/exotic-boarding-and-planning

## IN EXTREME HEAT

- Never leave a pet in a car. Temperatures rise quickly, even with the windows down. Call 911 if you see a pet or a child in a hot car.
- Give pets plenty of water and shade, and avoid exercising them outside on very hot days.
- When the city opens cooling centres, some welcome pets. Call 311 to find the nearest one; the city's online finder only works while cooling centres are open.

## IF YOUR PET IS HURT

- Go to an emergency hospital and ring on the way: https://nyc-animal-rescue.pages.dev/guides/animal-emergency
- The American Red Cross has a free Pet First Aid app with step-by-step first aid for common emergencies: search “Red Cross Pet First Aid” in your phone's app store. It is a guide while you get help, not a substitute for a vet.
