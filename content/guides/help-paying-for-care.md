# Help paying for veterinary care

_Grants, funds and low-cost options when you cannot afford treatment._

> Financial aid, low-cost care, insurance & food help (exotic-specific)

Why this tab: exotic vet care costs 2-3x standard pet care, far fewer clinics treat exotics, and most aid programs are written for cats and dogs. Below are the programs that actually cover exotics (birds, rabbits, small mammals, reptiles), plus the general funds that don't exclude them. Apply to SEVERAL at once - funds are limited and run out. Grants are paid to the veterinarian, not to you. Confirm eligibility and current status before relying on any single one.

## EXOTIC-SPECIFIC vet-bill funds (rare - these actually name exotics):

- AMC (Schwarzman Animal Medical Center) - Honey Bunny and Rou Fund: subsidized SPECIALTY care specifically for RABBITS (and German Shepherds) of owners with limited means. One pet per household per 12 months. Pet must first be seen by an AMC vet; apply within 5 business days of starting treatment; tri-state residency (NY/NJ/CT); no outstanding AMC balance; can't hold another AMC grant. funds@amcny.org, (212) 838-8100, amcny.org/financial-assistance.
- AMC - Kiki White Umbrella Cockatoo Avian Fund: subsidizes specialty care for ALL BIRDS of families with limited means. Same rules as above (AMC exam first, 5-business-day deadline, tri-state, one pet per household per 12 months). This is one of the very few bird-specific vet funds anywhere. funds@amcny.org.
- AMC - Charlie's Patient Assistance Fund (emergency care, any species), the Buddy Fund (cancer, any species), the Monty and Minny Fund (rehabilitation), and SAVE (owners 65+): all open to exotic pets under the same eligibility rules. Start with AMC's Eligibility Application at amcny.org/financial-assistance (income threshold about $50k/household).

## GENERAL vet-bill funds that cover exotics (birds, rabbits, guinea pigs, etc.):

- RedRover Relief - urgent/life-threatening care grants (avg ~$250); explicitly assists birds, rabbits and guinea pigs, not just cats/dogs; household income under ~$60k; paid to the vet. redrover.org/relief, (916) 429-2457. Also has a domestic-violence/pets program.
- The Pet Fund - non-urgent / chronic conditions for domestic animals (contact BEFORE treatment). info@thepetfund.com, (916) 443-6007, thepetfund.com.
- Frankie's Friends - lifesaving emergency or specialty care; pet already seen by a vet with a good prognosis. frankiesfriends.org/apply-for-assistance.
- The Mosby Foundation, Brown Dog Foundation, Onyx & Breezy Foundation - additional illness/injury funds that consider companion animals broadly; read each fund's species scope before applying.
- Waggle - vet-bill crowdfunding, 100% to the vet (waggle.org). Dial 211 or nyc.gov/211 for a live operator with current local assistance programs.

## For RESCUES (not individuals):

- AMC to the Rescue - subsidized specialty care for animals in the care of rescue groups whose health blocks adoption (amcny.org/amc-to-the-rescue). AMC also gives a 20% specialty-services discount to any 501(c)(3) animal nonprofit that presents an authorization letter on its letterhead + its IRS determination letter.

## Payment plans (accepted at most NYC vets, incl. exotics practices):

- CareCredit - healthcare credit card; carecredit.com or (800) 677-0718. Scratchpay - simple plans, soft credit check; scratchpay.com. Many aid funds require you to try/deny CareCredit or Scratchpay first.

## LOW-COST exotic care (few and far between - most low-cost clinics do NOT see exotics):

- Sean Casey Animal Rescue (153 East 3rd St, Brooklyn 11218; (718) 436-5163) runs low-cost vaccine/microchip clinics and takes in exotics; ask what species/services are covered.
- The Center for Avian & Exotic Medicine (562 Columbus Ave; (212) 501-8750) - not free, but the dedicated exotics day-practice; ask about payment plans + wellness packages to spread cost.
- Rabbit and bird rescues in the main directory (Rabbit Rescue & Rehab, All About Rabbits Rescue, Bunnies & Beyond, Northeast Avian Rescue) keep vetted rabbit/avian-savvy vet lists and can point to the most affordable exotic vets.
- ACC (nycacc.org / 311) provides some low-cost services and takes in small animals; its Community Pets program offers keep-your-pet support - ask whether it extends to your species.

## EXOTIC PET INSURANCE (plan ahead - does NOT cover pre-existing conditions, so start while the animal is young/healthy):

- Nationwide (petinsurance.com, (877) 738-7874) - the main insurer that covers birds, reptiles and many small mammals; the largest participating exotic-vet network. Ask specifically for its Avian & Exotic plan.
- Pet Assure (petassure.com) - not insurance but a veterinary DISCOUNT plan (usually 25% off in-house services) that covers virtually ANY species incl. exotics, with no pre-existing-condition exclusion; only works at participating vets - confirm your exotic vet accepts it.
- Some mainstream insurers (ASPCA Pet Health Insurance, others) cover certain small mammals/rabbits but not all exotics - always confirm species eligibility in writing before enrolling. Exotic wellness add-ons can cover beak/nail trims and routine exotic exams.
- Ask any insurer, in writing: which species you cover, how you define a pre-existing condition, and whether a curable condition can be covered later. Get 2-3 quotes and read the exclusions.

## FOOD & SUPPLY help (species-appropriate):

How to use aid effectively: get a written itemized estimate + proof of need (SNAP/Medicaid/income), then apply to MULTIPLE programs the same day. For an AMC fund, the pet must be seen at AMC and you must apply within 5 business days of starting treatment - so raise it with AMC staff immediately, not after the fact.

- Rescue Chewy wishlists / supply drives (e.g. Bunnies & Beyond) and rescue care pages provide correct-diet guidance - rabbits need unlimited grass hay, guinea pigs need vitamin C + a companion, many 'pet-store' foods are harmful.
- ACC food pantry (Bronx Resource Center) + the ASPCA / Food Bank For NYC partnership (250+ pantries citywide via foodbanknyc.org) distribute pet food - ask whether they stock rabbit/small-animal/bird food specifically.
- Feeding Pets of the Homeless (petsofthehomeless.org) and PAWS NY (pawsny.org, (212) 203-4760) help people in crisis or with mobility limits keep and feed their animals; ask about exotics.
