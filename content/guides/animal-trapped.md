# An animal is trapped

_Stuck in a drain, a wall, a pipe, a car engine or under a train: call 911 if it is in danger, 311 if it is not, and why to leave a healthy raccoon alone._

The Mayor’s Office of Animal Welfare told us, in writing in November 2026, what to do when an animal is trapped: call 911 if it is in danger, and call 311 if it is not, at any hour. If either does not help, email the Mayor’s Office of Animal Welfare at animalwelfare@cityhall.nyc.gov so it can follow up. It also pointed us to 311’s page on reporting animal abuse, https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-02224 . The rest of this page comes from 311's page on injured or sick animals, https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01789 , 311's page on raccoons, https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-02350 , and NYC Health's page on dog bites, https://www.nyc.gov/site/doh/health/health-topics/dog-bites.page , with what has happened in real rescues.

## If the animal is in danger right now, call 911

- If an animal is trapped and could die or be badly hurt, call 911. Say exactly where it is and what kind of animal it is.
- If 911 does not help, email the Mayor’s Office of Animal Welfare at animalwelfare@cityhall.nyc.gov so it can follow up.
- In rescues reported in the news, officers from the NYPD's Emergency Service Unit came to free the animal: a kitten in a Staten Island storm drain, https://amny.com/news/nypd-rescues-kitten-staten-island-drain , a dog under a subway train in Manhattan, https://abc7ny.com/post/nypd-officers-rescue-distressed-dog-beneath-subway-train-manhattan-nyc/18066501/ , and a cat inside a car engine, https://www.foxnews.com/us/elite-nypd-squad-rescues-cat-trapped-in-cars-engine . These are news reports, not a published city rule.

## If it is not in danger right now, call 311

- A sick or injured stray dog or cat: “During business hours, ACC accepts reports of sick or injured stray dogs, puppies, cats, and kittens.” 311 takes these calls between 10am and 6pm, except on city holidays.
- Outside those hours, the Mayor’s Office of Animal Welfare still says to call 311, and if that does not help, to email animalwelfare@cityhall.nyc.gov so it can follow up.
- An animal in a park: “You can report an animal in a park to NYC Parks”, through 311.
- A common bird or small wild animal, such as a pigeon or a squirrel: Animal Care Centers of NYC (ACC) will not pick it up, but takes it if you bring it to an ACC centre. Or call a licensed wildlife rehabber: [Found injured or orphaned wildlife](/guides/found-wildlife).
- Large wildlife, such as a deer or a goose, or a sick or injured raccoon, skunk, opossum or bat: report it to ACC through 311 during business hours.
- Your own pet: “Animal Care Centers of NYC (ACC) does not respond to reports of sick or injured pets.” If it is hurt, take it to a vet: [Animal emergencies and poison control](/guides/animal-emergency).

## A raccoon near your home

- The Mayor’s Office of Animal Welfare, in writing, November 2026: “For sightings of raccoons, please do not call 311, 911, DEC or “a professional” unless the raccoon is clearly injured or sick. Raccoons picked up by authorities or pest control companies are automatically euthanized because they are a rabies vector species, even if they are perfectly healthy. In other words, if you see a raccoon (even during the day, which just means they are hungry), please leave them alone.”
- Take away whatever food or shelter is drawing it in.
- If you are bitten or scratched by a raccoon, wash the wound and see your doctor as soon as possible. Report it to 311.
