# Found kittens outside

_Check if they are hurt, then wait and watch for the mother. What to do if she does not come back._

What the city tells New Yorkers to do when they find kittens outdoors. The quotations are from 311's page on stray animals, which gives the guidance of Animal Care Centers of NYC (ACC): https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-02300 . ACC's own page on outdoor kittens has a step-by-step chart: https://www.nycacc.org/programs/community-cats/

## First, call someone who knows kittens

- Before you move, feed or keep kittens, call a kitten rescue or ACC and ask what to do. How old the kittens are decides what they need. Kitten nurseries that take them: [Cats needing specialist help](/guides/cats-with-special-needs).
- Kitten Lady's guides show what caring for kittens involves, so you know what to ask: https://www.kittenlady.org/kitten-care . Use them with a rescue's help, not instead of it.

## If a kitten is hurt or very sick

- It needs help now. Go to [Animal emergencies and poison control](/guides/animal-emergency), or call 311 to reach Animal Care Centers of NYC (ACC).

## What the city says about waiting for the mother

- 311 and ACC say: “Do not separate stray or feral kittens from their mother. You should always monitor the kittens from a distance for a few hours in case the mother cat returns.”
- Watch from far away. Do not take the kittens home while you wait.

## If the mother does not come back

- “ACC accepts reports of orphaned kittens. You may also bring an orphaned kitten to an ACC location.” Call 311, or ACC on (212) 788-4000. ACC asks you to call before you arrive with a stray animal.
- ACC's care centres are closed on New Year's Day, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day.
- Very young kittens that still need bottle feeding need care around the clock. Kitten nurseries and rescues that take them are in [Cats needing specialist help](/guides/cats-with-special-needs).

## Grown cats

- ACC “does not capture or accept reports of healthy stray or feral cats. Bringing in a stray cat may not be in their best interest if the cat appears healthy and thriving in the community.”
- A cat with the tip of one ear cut off has been spayed or neutered.
- To stop more litters, the mother cat can be trapped, spayed and returned to where she lives. How it works, and who helps for free: [Community cats, colonies and starting TNR](/guides/starting-tnr).
