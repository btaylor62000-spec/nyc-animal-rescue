# Lost or found a cat

_What to do first, where to post, and who helps with searching._

## LOST A CAT - do all of these fast (80% of lost cats are found within a mile - usually much closer, hiding)

- Petco Love Lost (petcolove.org/lost) - FREE national database with FACIAL-RECOGNITION photo matching. Upload a clear face photo, report as LOST, print the auto-generated flyer, check every few hours.
- ACC lost/found - nycacc.org/services/lost-and-found - file a Lost Pet Report, search the Found database, and VISIT all open Care Centers in person with a photo + proof of ownership (shelters don't always post daily).
- If chipped: contact the microchip company immediately, report the cat lost, confirm your contact info is current.
- Post to NYC lost-and-found networks: PawBoost, Pet FBI, Nextdoor, Craigslist lost-and-found, and location-based Facebook groups ('Lost and Found Pets of [neighborhood/NYC]'). Make ONE shareable flyer image with photo + last-seen location/time + your number.
- CAT-SPECIFIC SEARCH (cats hide, they don't run far): search silently at DUSK/NIGHT with a flashlight + carrier + smelly food; check under cars, porches, shrubs, basements, alleys, dumpsters. An indoor cat that bolted is usually within a few houses, hiding and silent for days.
- Indoor escape? Search your OWN home first - behind the stove, in cabinets, closets, under furniture. Put their litter box / a worn t-shirt outside the door; the scent can draw them back.
- Ask the people who walk your block: mail carriers, delivery drivers, supers, door attendants, restaurant basement staff, park workers. Post flyers at vets, pet stores, and building mailboxes (get permission on private property).
- Do NOT chase a sighted cat - it makes them bolt. Sit, talk softly, offer food, and consider a humane trap (borrow one: https://nyc-animal-rescue.pages.dev/guides/low-cost-clinics-and-trap-banks).

## FOUND A CAT

- Get it SCANNED for a microchip at any vet or shelter (free) - the fastest route to the owner. Also check for a collar/tag and an EAR-TIP (a tipped ear = a TNR'd community cat with a caretaker, not a lost pet - leave it be).
- Report as FOUND on Petco Love Lost + file a found report with ACC. Search local Facebook lost-and-found groups + Nextdoor for a matching 'lost' post.
- SICK or INJURED stray/community cat? Call 311, or ACC on (212) 788-4000, to report a sick or injured stray cat: ACC takes these reports during business hours. You can also email CommunityPets@nycacc.org. Don't handle an aggressive/fearful cat.
- Healthy, friendly cat? Don't assume it's a stray - it may be an indoor/outdoor neighbor's cat. ACC does NOT accept healthy stray cats; post found flyers + check for a chip first. If truly abandoned, contact a borough rescue.
- NY stray-hold law: found pets generally must be reported to animal control and held a minimum number of days before adoption - check with ACC.

## MICROCHIP REGISTRIES (register the chip - a chip that isn't registered can't reunite anyone)

- 24PetWatch (now at petplace.com/pet-microchip-registry; 24Petwatch accounts moved to PetPlace) - North America's largest registry, FREE registration, any chip brand.
- Petco Love Lost - free, doubles as the lost/found database above.
- Don't know the chip company? Look up the number at found.org/owners or the AAHA Universal Lookup (petmicrochiplookup.org).
- Where to GET a cat chipped cheaply: ACC, HSNY, and most low-cost clinics (https://nyc-animal-rescue.pages.dev/guides/low-cost-clinics-and-trap-banks) do microchipping at the spay/neuter visit.

## BEHAVIOR / SOCIALIZATION - part A: feral/shy cats & kittens (make them adoptable)

- Urban Cat League Taming Toolkit (urbancatleague.org/taming-toolkit) - THE NYC resource: written crash-course + 'Taming Feral Kittens & Cats' video + taming blog. Share it with adopters/fosters to prevent back-sliding.
- Rule of thumb: kittens under 8 weeks socialize easily; 8 weeks-6 months takes longer but works; adult ferals sometimes too. Work at feeding time when they're hungry, start with head/shoulders, use a large dog crate or a bathroom, hand-feed to build trust.
- UCL workshops: Taming Feral Street Kittens, Bottle-Feeding Neonatals, Building Winter Shelters, Incorporating a 501(c)(3), and the TNR Certification workshop (15,000+ certified).
- Kitten Lady (kittenlady.org) - free how-to videos on neonatal/kitten care + socialization. Cornell Feline Health Center - vet-backed health and behavior articles: https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics

## BEHAVIOR / SOCIALIZATION - part B: OWNED-cat problems (these are the #1 surrender triggers - solvable!)

- FREE National Cat Behavior Helpline - email felinehelpline@gmail.com with your contact info + a one-line issue ('cat not using litter box'); free counseling aimed at keeping cats in their homes.
- FIRST STEP for any sudden behavior change (esp. litter box) = a VET VISIT to rule out a medical cause (UTI, kidney, pain). Behavior fixes won't work if it's medical.
- Litter box (10%+ of cats): one box per cat PLUS one extra, scoop daily, unscented clumping litter about 2in deep, big uncovered box, quiet accessible spot. Never punish or place the cat in the box. Spraying (vertical, small amount) is marking, not a box problem - neuter fixes about 90%.
- Cat-to-cat aggression / introductions: separate resources (multiple bowls, beds, boxes, perches), go SLOW with a new cat (scent-swap, then feed on opposite sides of a door, then visual, then supervised). Feliway pheromone diffuser can lower tension. Don't soothe an aroused cat - give space (redirected aggression can turn on you).
- Scratching: it's normal (stretching/marking) - provide tall sturdy posts + horizontal cardboard, don't declaw (harmful; alternatives exist). Reward using the post.
- Authoritative free libraries: ASPCA Common Cat Behavior Issues (aspca.org/pet-care/cat-care), Pam Johnson-Bennett (catbehaviorassociates.com), Best Friends, Cornell Feline Health Center (https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center).
- For serious/persistent cases: ask your vet for a referral to a board-certified veterinary behaviorist (Dip ACVB) or Certified Applied Animal Behaviorist (CAAB).

Prevention beats everything: a registered microchip + keeping cats indoors is the single strongest safety net. A loud noise or sudden movement can send an outdoor cat running - think of a cat outside as a toddler alone.
