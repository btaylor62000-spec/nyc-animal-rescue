# When a pet dies

_What the city allows: cremation, burial and sanitation pickup, what to do first if it bit someone, and help at the end of life._

Losing a pet is hard. Take the time you need. This page says what the city allows you to do when a pet dies at home, from 311's page on a dead animal on private property, https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-03451 , and the Department of Sanitation's page, https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/get-rid-of/animals.page

## FIRST: DID IT BITE OR SCRATCH SOMEONE, OR COULD IT HAVE HAD RABIES?

- Do not put dead animals that may have been rabid in the trash.
- If the animal showed signs of rabies, call 911.
- If the animal bit a person before it died, call 311 or report it online.

## YOUR CHOICES

- Cremation: “You can bring the remains of a deceased pet or another dead animal to an Animal Care Centers of NYC (ACC) drop-off location for cremation.” ACC charges a fee for each animal. Contact ACC on (212) 788-4000 for details, including how to pay.
- Burial: “If you are the property owner, you can bury deceased pets or other animals on your private property.”
- Sanitation pickup: place the body in a heavy-duty black plastic bag or a double plastic bag. “You must tape a note to the bag stating its contents (for example, "dead dog" or "dead cat").” Put the bag inside a bin before you put it out with your regular trash.

## IF YOU NEED HELP MOVING YOUR PET

- AmbuVet's pet ambulance also carries animals that have died: https://www.ambuvet.com

## IF YOUR PET IS VERY ILL

- Lap of Love gives in-home hospice and end-of-life veterinary care across NYC: https://lapoflove.com
- Going into hospital yourself? PAWS NY's Hospital Respite Program helps people who are hospitalized or in rehab care for their pets: https://pawsny.org

## A DEAD ANIMAL ON THE STREET, OR SEVERAL DEAD BIRDS

- A dead animal on a sidewalk or street: report it to the Department of Sanitation through 311: https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-02245 . If it is blocking highway traffic, call 911.
- If you are seeing clusters of dead birds, fish, or other wildlife, call 311.
- To move a dead bird, use plastic bags or disposable rubber gloves, and wash your hands with warm soapy water afterward.
