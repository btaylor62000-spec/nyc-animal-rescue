# Rehoming a rabbit, bird, reptile or small animal

_Never release a domestic animal outdoors. Where to surrender responsibly._

> Owner support & surrender prevention (exotic-specific)

The single most important message: NEVER release a domestic animal to the wild. Domestic rabbits, king/white pigeons, red-eared sliders, goldfish and farmed birds released outdoors almost always die (predators, cars, starvation, cold) and can spread disease or disrupt native ecosystems. There is always a better path than release, dumping, or handing an animal to a pet store 'feeder' pipeline.

## Why these surrenders happen - and the humane framing:

- 'Easter bunny' rabbits: bought as impulse/holiday gifts, then surrendered when the ~10-year commitment, indoor housing, rabbit-savvy vet care and spay/neuter needs become clear. Rabbits are fragile, dislike being picked up, and are seldom suitable as children's 'starter pets.'
- Red-eared slider turtles: sold tiny and cheap, they grow large, live decades, and are invasive/illegal to release. Rescues are overwhelmed with them; rehoming can take a long time.
- 'Teacup'/'mini' pigs: marketed as staying small; they grow into large animals that are illegal to keep in most NYC residential zoning and need farmed-animal sanctuary placement.
- Roosters & backyard poultry: chicks bought at markets/feed stores turn out to be roosters (illegal/impractical in most NYC housing) and need sanctuary, not release.

## Keep-the-animal help (surrender prevention):

- Rabbit Rescue & Rehab offers surrender-prevention coaching, care techniques and adopter-screening help (nyc.metro.rabbits@gmail.com) - most owners who felt they had to give up a bun find care can be made far easier.
- Reenie's Rabbit Rescue offers bonding, boarding, grooming and advice (845-591-7751).
- Bunnies & Beyond, All About Rabbits Rescue, NYTTS and Empty Cages Collective all provide species-appropriate care education (housing, diet, bonding) aimed at keeping animals in their homes.

## Safe surrender paths (never release-to-wild):

Species-appropriate food & supply help

Chewy wishlists and rescue supply drives (e.g. Bunnies & Beyond's Chewy wishlist) and rescue care pages provide correct diet guidance - e.g. rabbits need unlimited grass hay (not a pellet-heavy diet), guinea pigs need vitamin C and a bonded companion, and many 'pet-store' foods are actively harmful. Ask the relevant rescue's care page before changing a diet.

- Rabbits/guinea pigs: Rabbit Rescue & Rehab, All About Rabbits Rescue, Bunnies & Beyond, Empty Cages Collective, or Animal Care Centers of NYC (nycacc.org). Screen adopters carefully if rehoming yourself (protect against 'feeder'/bait uses).
- Turtles/reptiles: NY Turtle & Tortoise Society (qanda@nytts.org), Earthlings NYC Turtle Team, or Stray Pride.
- Birds: Northeast Avian Rescue, Long Island Parrot Society placement program; feral/domestic pigeons -\> NYCPRC.
- Farmed animals/poultry/pigs: Woodstock Farm Sanctuary (AnimalHelp@woodstocksanctuary.org) or Catskill Animal Sanctuary (confirm capacity first).
- Any species you can't place: Animal Care Centers of NYC is the open-admission safety net (nycacc.org / 311).
